# A brief, a bit, and a revision

This example uses a fictional brief and simulated feedback. It was written to
illustrate the workflow; it is not a user's biography or an audience evaluation.

## Brief

> I bought a label maker to get organized. I labelled the drawer full of cables
> 'miscellaneous'. I'm still not sure which cable does what.
>
> Give me three angles, then a short bit. Quiet and self-deprecating, no profanity,
> and no extra personal incidents. Keep the first sentence.

## Angles

1. **Naming the problem feels like solving it.** “Before, it was a mess. Now it's
   miscellaneous. Apparently all I needed was a longer word.”
2. **The label is for an imaginary visitor.** “If someone opens that drawer, I
   want them to think this was a decision.”
3. **A specific machine meets a vague person.** “A very precise machine. A very
   vague plan.”

The first angle gives the draft a through-line: naming something can feel like
doing something about it. This is an editorial choice, not a funniness score.

## First bit

I bought a label maker to get organized. I labelled the drawer full of cables
'miscellaneous'. I'm still not sure which cable does what.

But the problem has a title now. Before, it was a mess. Now it's miscellaneous.
Apparently all I needed was a longer word.

If someone opens that drawer, I want them to think this was a decision. Not a
good decision. Just one with a label.

I could sort the cables properly, but then I'd have to admit the label maker
wasn't the part that did the organizing. It was just the part I could buy.

100 words: about 40–50 seconds before pauses at an assumed 120–150 words/minute.
Actual delivery needs a read-aloud.

## Simulated feedback

> Keep the first sentence and facts. Lose the longer-word joke and the imaginary
> visitor. Make it quieter and shorter, with less explanation.

## Revision

I bought a label maker to get organized. I labelled the drawer full of cables
'miscellaneous'. I'm still not sure which cable does what.

I could sort them properly. Then I'd have to admit that was the task.

For now, I know exactly where the miscellaneous is.

The revision removes the two specified passages and returns to the label at the
end. It keeps the opening and supplied facts. It is 47 words; shorter does not
necessarily mean funnier. The writer can retain either version or combine them.

## A connected routine

Add two facts to the fictional brief: “I bought a notebook to be more productive.
The first item on my list was 'buy notebook'” and “I put a reminder in my calendar
to relax.” Ask for a connected routine of roughly two minutes.

I bought a label maker to get organized. I labelled the drawer full of cables
'miscellaneous'. I'm still not sure which cable does what.

I could sort them properly. Then I'd have to admit that was the task.

For now, I know exactly where the miscellaneous is.

I think that's what I like about getting organized. There's a little bit at the
beginning where you get to buy something, before any of your habits are involved.

I bought a notebook to be more productive. The first item on my list was 'buy
notebook'. It's nice to start with something you're qualified for.

After that, a list gets quite demanding. It starts bringing up things you haven't
done. I don't think that's the same supportive atmosphere.

At least a notebook lets you close it. A calendar wants to know when.

I put a reminder in my calendar to relax. That's where I may have misunderstood
the assignment. If I miss it, am I behind on relaxing? Should I catch up at the
weekend, or would that be overtime?

I could leave that bit of the calendar empty. But then I'd have to sit there
with no written explanation for what I'm doing.

Maybe I could call it 'miscellaneous'.

Then the cables and I would finally have something in common. Neither of us
would have to explain what we're for.

The routine connects naming, listing, and scheduling. The final callback puts the
speaker in the cables' position. At 228 words, it is about 91–114 seconds before
pauses using the same planning assumption. No delivery or audience trial was run.
