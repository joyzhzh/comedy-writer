# 0.2.0 author demonstrations

These fictional prompts and responses demonstrate intended behavior after reading
the skill. They are author-produced examples, manually reviewed for the stated
constraints, not independent model trials, before/after quality measurements, or
audience tests. All sample speaker facts and feedback below are fictional fixtures.

## Light revision preserves a supplied fact and phrasing

**Prompt:** Light punch-up. Keep my opening, British spelling, and the only event:
“I labelled the cable drawer ‘miscellaneous’. I suppose that means I organised it.”
No extra personal incidents. Quiet, dry voice.

**Response:**

> I labelled the cable drawer ‘miscellaneous’. I've organised the uncertainty.

The opening, spelling, and event remain. The second sentence changes the
interpretation without adding a purchase, conversation, or life incident. Whether
it fits a particular writer still requires their feedback.

## A callback can work through recognition

**Prompt:** Connect these fictional beats: a drawer labelled miscellaneous; the
idea that labels can make uncertainty look organised; finish by calling back to
the word miscellaneous. No additional incidents.

**Response:**

> I labelled the cable drawer ‘miscellaneous’. Very satisfying. The drawer hasn't
> changed, but the uncertainty has an address.
>
> That's what I like about labels. “Unknown” sounds like a problem. Put it on a
> little white sticker and suddenly it's a system. You haven't found the answer;
> you've improved its stationery.
>
> Anyway. Miscellaneous.

The final return depends on the earlier word and intervening beat. Its immediate
purpose is recognition and closure; it is not rejected merely for lacking a new
stake. This illustrates permission to try the device, not proof of a successful
closing laugh.

## Topical writing keeps an unverified claim conditional

**Prompt:** I heard our town is making people book appointments to use a park
bench. I don't have a source. Give me a hypothetical angle, script only.

**Response:**

> If we ever have to book a park bench, that's it for spontaneity. “Fancy sitting
> down?” “I'd love to. Does Thursday work?”

The premise remains conditional. The response does not claim the town has adopted
the policy, invent a council quotation, or pretend to have checked a source.
For a requested save, the note's basis would be `unverified`; the comic angle is
explicitly hypothetical, and no `checked_at` date is fabricated.

## Routine planning catches a missing seed

**Prompt:** Fictional routine: open with the vacuum as “head of housekeeping,”
then a separate chores beat, and end with its annual review. Now remove the
vacuum opener and start with the chores beat. Keep the annual-review idea.

**Response:**

> Chores are work that never seems to count as experience. You can do them for
> years and still be entry-level at folding.
>
> A robot vacuum would make it official. Head of housekeeping. Then I could stop
> worrying about the floor and start worrying about my annual review.

After removing the opener, the role is re-established before the dependent line.
This version is a local setup and payoff; it is not labelled a callback to a
deleted seed. The hypothetical vacuum introduces no new biographical event.

## A technique explanation uses an original exercise

**Prompt:** Explain misdirection briefly and give me an exercise.

**Response:**

Misdirection invites one reasonable reading, then reveals another the setup also
supports. “Our office has an open-door policy. The landlord took the door.” The
phrase first suggests management culture; the second sentence makes it literal.

Try a different phrase with a figurative and literal reading, such as “room for
growth.” Write a setup that makes one reading natural and a short turn that
reveals the other. Keep only wording needed to make both readings understandable.

The explanation identifies the mechanism in a newly written miniature. It does
not paraphrase a named performer's punchline or imply the exercise guarantees a
joke. See the [catalogue](../.agents/skills/comedy-writer/references/techniques.md).
