# References for Comedy Writer 0.2.0

This is the complete source list used or linked by this edition's writing,
study, reuse, and access guidance, organized by purpose. It is not a complete
bibliography of comedy, a claim that every linked work was read in full, or a
ranking of teachers. Catalogue entries are study leads. Access observations were
checked in September 2026 and can change. Dates marked “n.d.” were not established.
The host's own skill documentation is linked separately in the repository README.

## Practitioner guidance

1. **Dean, Greg.** *Greg Dean's Step By Step to Stand-Up Comedy PDF Workbook.*
   PDF carrying 2016 and 2024 copyright notices; exact publication day not
   established. [Workbook](https://stand-upcomedy.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/New-SBS-Bonus-Workbook-2024-PDF-final-2.pdf).
   Consulted: chapter 2, printed pp. 1–3, and chapter 3, pp. 4–9. Supports the
   simplified assumption/alternative-interpretation prompt. The complete book
   and course were not read for this edition.
2. **Roberts, Rik.** (2015, March 6). *Live Comedy Writing Session – Idea to
   Premise*, School of Laughs, episode 036.
   [Article and show notes](https://www.schooloflaughs.com/036-live-comedy-writing-session-idea-premise-podcast/).
   Consulted the written expand/condense/define discussion and the limits of
   judging before a performance; audio not played. Informs distinct premise
   options and provisional selection. Neuroscience claims are not adopted.
3. **Corley, Jerry.** (n.d.). *Trying to Write Jokes, but Feeling Stuck?*
   Stand Up Comedy Clinic.
   [Article](https://www.standupcomedyclinic.com/trying-to-write-jokes-but-feeling-stuck/).
   Consulted the discussion of attitude, details, analogy, and first drafts.
   Informs optional drafting prompts; no complete course was studied.
4. **August, John, and Craig Mazin**, hosts; **Mike Birbiglia**, guest.
   (Episode May 27, 2025; transcript May 28). *Scriptnotes, episode 688: Writing
   Jokes with Mike Birbiglia*.
   [Official transcript](https://johnaugust.com/2025/scriptnotes-episode-688-writing-jokes-with-mike-birbiglia-transcript).
   Consulted the transitions/first-time-listener discussion and the interview's
   returning reporter-question example. Supports transition work and a bounded
   callback study. Conversation text is not a complete performance; the guest's
   account of altering his own stories does not justify changing a user's facts.
5. **Dean, Greg.** (n.d.). *Comedy Terms*.
   [Glossary](https://stand-upcomedy.com/glossary/).
   Consulted entries for callback, tag, and act-out. The glossary's callback
   definition is narrower than this skill's broader return-to-earlier-material
   convention. Historical origin stories and performance-rate claims are not
   used. Neither wording establishes what every successful callback must do.

## Attribution limits

The sixteen-item catalogue, six revision prompts, fact and voice preservation,
edit-distance choices, single-conversation interaction, source-note fields,
snapshot format, and reduced beat map are project synthesis or implementation
choices. Some draw on the bounded sources above; they are not a validated comedy
standard. End-word placement and 120–150 words/minute remain disclosed editing
and planning assumptions. Recognition-only callbacks are allowed by this skill's
working convention, not presented as a conclusion established by every source.

There is no benchmark vault of verified “good jokes” in this package. The
catalogue and illustrations support writing experiments; writer feedback and
reported audience observations remain separate from the agent's own judgment.
