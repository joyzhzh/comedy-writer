# Stand-up technique catalogue

This is a practical, non-exhaustive menu. Broad mechanisms, writing devices,
character choices, and delivery tools overlap; the rows are not sixteen separate
scientifically established mechanisms. Names and uses vary between teachers.
Use one relevant option, then listen to the line in context. No technique promises
laughter. The [bibliography](sources.md) identifies source support and project
synthesis; it does not attribute this whole taxonomy to any single teacher.

The short examples below were written for this edition, rather than transcribed
from a performance. They are exercises, not audience-tested jokes or claims of
global originality. When writing for someone, fit their facts and voice instead
of assigning them the fictional speaker's experiences.

| Technique | Working meaning and when it may help | Original miniature exercise |
|---|---|---|
| Expectation violation | Establish an understandable expectation, then depart from it. The departure still needs a connection the listener can follow. | “My smart kettle won't boil until I agree to its terms. I thought I was making tea; apparently I'm joining a company.” |
| Incongruity | Bring together frames that do not normally belong together. A surprising combination alone may still need a speaker's attitude. | “The meditation app has a leaderboard. I'm third at letting go.” |
| Reversal | Exchange a familiar direction, role, or outcome. Useful when the relationship is clearer than a word puzzle. | “I'm training my fitness app to expect less.” |
| Misdirection / reinterpretation | Encourage one reasonable reading, then reveal another that the setup also supports. Do not depend on arbitrary withheld facts. | “Our office has an open-door policy. The landlord took the door.” |
| Specificity | Replace an abstraction with a detail that changes what the listener pictures or understands. Detail is a tool, not a quota. | Replace “cheap umbrella” with “an umbrella that folds itself whenever it detects rain.” |
| Analogy | Map one situation onto another with a shared relationship. Extend only as far as that relationship remains useful. | “My inbox is an animal shelter. I can't take them all home, but they keep looking at me.” |
| Exaggeration | Magnify an established trait or consequence; make the imaginary extension legible. | “My laptop fan has requested clearance to land.” |
| Escalation | Develop successive consequences under the same premise's logic. Louder wording alone is not development. | A stretch app sends a reminder, schedules a meeting, then marks the speaker absent from their own legs. |
| Rule of three / pattern breaking | Establish a short pattern and use a later item to redirect it. Three is a familiar option, not a required count. | “For the hike I packed water, snacks, and a convincing reason to turn back.” |
| Wordplay / double meaning | Use an actual ambiguity in wording or sound. Check that this audience can hear both readings. | “I work well under pressure. That's why I open soda very slowly.” |
| Contrast / juxtaposition | Place two revealing positions or images close together. The contrast can carry attitude without a separate twist. | “The retreat bans phones. Check-in requires the app.” |
| Persona / self-implication / status | Let the speaker's self-image, desire, or social position shape the joke. This is a character lens, not compulsory self-mockery. | “I'm the family tech expert. I know which nephew to call.” |
| Tags | Add short follow-up punchlines to an established setup. Assess each in context; repetition can also serve rhythm or a pattern. | “I gave my to-do list a day off. It hired a temp. The temp scheduled my guilt.” |
| Callbacks | Return to an established beat after intervening material. Recognition, continuity, or changed meaning can justify it. | Seed: the robot vacuum is “head of housekeeping.” Later, after a separate chores beat: “I can't relax; housekeeping is doing my annual review.” |
| Act-outs | Briefly portray a voice, body, exchange, or action instead of only describing it. A writing suggestion cannot establish performance timing. | Portray the self-checkout requesting a witness for one banana; make the speaker's small response clear. |
| Transitions / story resumption | Give enough connective information to move between bits or return to an interrupted story. This is a routine tool, not necessarily a punchline. | “Labelling the boxes was easy. Keeping track of the label maker was the problem.” |

To teach a requested technique: explain it plainly, identify the relevant part of
an original example, then give a small exercise. If the writer wants only a list,
give the list. Keep performer study about structure and choices; do not turn a
source routine into a bank of lines to paraphrase.
